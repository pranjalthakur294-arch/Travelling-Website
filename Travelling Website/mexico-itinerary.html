Day 10
Days 1–3 · Mexico City
The Capital of Everything
Mexico City is one of the great megacities — 22 million people, 160 museums, the best food scene in the Americas, and an Aztec pyramid under the main square. It defies comprehension and rewards surrender.
08:00
Zócalo & Templo Mayor at sunriseThe ancient Aztec ceremonial centre was discovered beneath the cathedral in 1978. Walk the excavated ruins, then look up at the Colonial cathedral built from its stones — 500 years of conquest in one glance.
11:00
Palacio Nacional murals — Diego RiveraThe greatest mural in the Americas covers the entire staircase wall. Rivera spent 25 years on it. You need at least one hour just to begin to see it properly.
14:00
Lunch at Mercado de San JuanThe city's best gourmet market. Try tlayudas, chapulines (toasted grasshoppers with lime — yes, really) and a michelada while standing at the counter.
16:30
Museo Frida Kahlo, CoyoacánThe Blue House where Frida was born, lived and died. Her studio, her personal objects, her pain — all preserved exactly as she left them. Book ahead; numbers are controlled.
09:00
Day 2: Teotihuacán pyramids (day trip)The Pyramid of the Sun is the third largest pyramid on Earth. Climb it at 9am before the heat is punishing. The Avenue of the Dead stretches two kilometres into the plain below you.
20:00
Dinner & mezcal in Roma NorteMexico City's most creative neighbourhood. Dinner at Contramar (grilled fish, book ahead), then mezcal at Bósforo — a tiny bar run by a mezcal philosopher.
Days 4–5 · Oaxaca
The Soul of Mexican Cuisine
Oaxaca is where Mexican food reaches its highest expression — seven moles, tlayudas, chapulines, mezcal from single-village distilleries. The Colonial centre is jade-green and baroque. The markets are overwhelming in the best way.
07:30
Mercado Benito Juárez at openingArrive before the heat. Stalls selling dried chillies, Oaxacan string cheese, tejate (ancient chocolate-maize drink) and insects with chilli salt. Buy everything. Eat standing.
10:00
Monte Albán — Zapotec ruinsA 2,500-year-old city levelled across a mountain top. The scale is staggering; the views of the Valley of Oaxaca are among the finest in Mexico.
14:00
Mole negro tasting lunchAt Casa Oaxaca restaurant — chef Alejandro Ruiz's mole negro takes three days to prepare. This is the dish that defines a civilization.
16:00
Mezcal palenque visit near MitlaA small family distillery using clay pot stills. Watch the agave roasting pits, smell the smoke, taste seven expressions straight from the still.
09:00
Day 5: Hierve el Agua — petrified waterfallsMineral-laden springs have created frozen limestone cascades over a valley. Swim in the cliff-edge infinity pools. Surreal, remote, extraordinary.
Days 6–8 · Yucatán Peninsula
Maya, Cenotes & Colonial Gold
The Yucatán is another country within a country — flat limestone jungle, underground rivers, Maya cities abandoned to the jungle, and Mérida, one of the finest Colonial cities in the Americas.
07:00
Chichén Itzá — first entryGet there the moment the gates open. By 10am, tour groups make the site feel like a theme park. At 7am, El Castillo pyramid belongs to you and the birds.
11:30
Cenote Ik KilA sacred Maya sinkhole — vines trailing to a perfect circle of turquoise water 30m below the jungle floor. Swim before the day-trippers arrive from Cancún.
15:00
Mérida — the White CityCheck in to a restored hacienda in the centro histórico. Mérida has the highest concentration of Colonial mansions in the Americas — and the best marquesitas (crispy crepes) in Mexico.
09:00
Day 7: Uxmal ruinsLess visited than Chichén Itzá but architecturally more refined. The Pyramid of the Magician has a rounded profile unlike any other structure in Mesoamerica.
10:00
Day 8: Rio Secreto underground riverStrap on a headlamp and wade through a 600m stretch of subterranean river through cathedral stalactite chambers. The silence underground is total.
Days 9–10 · Tulum
Cliffside Ruins & Caribbean Blue
Tulum is the only Maya site built on a cliff overlooking the Caribbean Sea. Swim below the ruins, explore jungle cenotes, and eat the freshest fish tacos of your life as the sun sets into the jungle behind you.
07:30
Tulum ruins at sunriseThe postcard image of Mexico — a Maya temple on a white cliff above turquoise Caribbean water. Arrive for opening; the light is gold and the beach below empty.
10:00
Cenote Dos Ojos — snorkellingTwo enormous connected cenotes in the jungle. Float through crystal-clear water over ancient limestone formations. One of the most beautiful places in Mexico — no hyperbole.
13:00
Fish tacos on the beach roadLa Eufemia or El Camello Jr — no tables, no menus, just perfect grilled fish, hand-pressed tortillas and cold Modelo under a palm roof. This is why you came.
16:00
Sian Ka'an Biosphere ReserveA UNESCO-protected wilderness of mangroves, lagoons and jungle. Boat through flooded Maya channels; spot flamingos, manatees and crocodiles as the sun drops.
20:00
Farewell dinner at HartwoodOpen-fire cooking in the jungle — no electricity, the menu changes daily based on what arrived from local farms that morning. Reserve the moment you land in Mexico.

Local tips

🌮 The best tacos in Mexico cost under 20 pesos. If a taco costs more than $5 USD, you're in the tourist version of Mexico. Walk one block further.
🥃 Mezcal is not tequila. It's made from over 30 agave species, often in tiny batches in clay pots. Sip it slowly — don't shoot it. The smoky ones aren't even the best ones.
🏛 Book Frida Kahlo Museum, Chichén Itzá early-entry and Hartwood restaurant months in advance. These fill completely.
🚌 ADO buses between cities are modern, punctual and safe — often better than flying when you factor in airport time. The overnight CDMX–Oaxaca bus is an institution.
🌊 In cenotes, wear reef-safe sunscreen only — or none at all. The water is an underground river system; whatever you put on your skin enters the aquifer.
