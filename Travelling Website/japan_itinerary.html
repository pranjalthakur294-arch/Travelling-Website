Day 10
Day 1 · Wednesday · Tokyo
Shibuya, Ramen & Neon Nights
Tokyo doesn't ease you in — it swallows you whole from the moment you exit the shinkansen. Start where the city's heartbeat is loudest: Shibuya Crossing and beyond.
11:00
Shibuya Crossing & SkyStand on the world's busiest pedestrian crossing, then take the elevator to Shibuya Sky for a 360° panorama of Tokyo stretching to Fuji on clear days.
13:30
Lunch at Ichiran RamenJapan's legendary solo-dining ramen chain. Fill in your flavour preferences card, take your private booth, and experience tonkotsu perfection.
15:30
Harajuku & Takeshita StreetJapan's most gloriously unhinged fashion district. Crepes, cosplay, and culture colliding on a single chaotic alley.
19:00
Shinjuku Golden GaiA warren of 200 tiny bars, each seating six. Pick one with a handwritten menu, order yakitori and whisky, and talk to everyone.
Day 2 · Thursday · Tokyo
Temples, Tsukiji & Teamlab
Old Tokyo and new Tokyo exist in the same breath. Ancient Asakusa at dawn, the finest sushi breakfast on earth, and digital art that dissolves the boundary between you and the universe.
07:00
Senso-ji Temple, AsakusaTokyo's oldest temple in the golden hour before the crowds arrive. The approach down Nakamise-dori is lined with snack stalls worth exploring.
09:30
Breakfast at Tsukiji Outer MarketThe world's best fish breakfast — tamagoyaki omelette, sea urchin on rice, and maguro (tuna) so fresh it barely touches the soy sauce.
14:00
teamLab Borderless (Azabudai Hills)The world's most visited digital art museum. Immersive light and sound environments that are genuinely unlike anything else on earth. Book ahead.
19:00
Izakaya dinner in EbisuA neighbourhood izakaya — grilled skewers, cold Sapporo, edamame, and the quiet pleasure of a Tokyo local table.
Day 3 · Friday · Tokyo
Akihabara, Yanaka & Sumo
Tokyo contains multitudes. Electric Town's neon madness, the preserved Edo-era lanes of Yanaka, and — if timing permits — the explosive theatre of sumo.
10:00
Akihabara Electric TownSeven floors of manga, anime, retro games and electronics. A sensory overload in the best possible sense. The maid cafés are entirely optional.
13:00
Yanaka neighbourhoodOld Shitamachi Tokyo — wooden shopfronts, family-run sembei shops and the quiet Yanaka Cemetery that blooms with sakura. Walk slowly here.
15:30
Ryogoku Sumo MuseumFree entry, fascinating history. If you're visiting during a tournament (January, May, September), spring for arena tickets — it's one of Japan's great sporting spectacles.
20:00
Omakase counter dinnerBook a 10-seat omakase counter in advance. The chef chooses — 12 courses of market-fresh nigiri served piece by piece. Budget ¥15,000–25,000 per person.
Day 4 · Saturday · Hakone
Fuji Views, Hot Springs & Ryokan
An hour from Tokyo, Hakone delivers the Japan of your imagination — volcanic valleys wreathed in mist, steaming outdoor baths, and Fuji hovering above the treeline.
08:30
Romancecar from ShinjukuOdakyu's iconic limited express — a panoramic window train that winds through mountains into Hakone in 85 minutes. Book the front seats.
11:00
Owakudani Volcanic ValleyCable car over the sulphur vents. Eat a kuro-tamago (black egg hard-boiled in volcanic springs) — legend promises it adds seven years to your life.
13:30
Lake Ashi & Fuji viewsBoard the pirate ship cruise across the caldera lake. On clear days, Fuji's perfect cone dominates the horizon above the torii gate of Hakone Shrine.
16:00
Ryokan check-in & onsenYour traditional inn includes yukata robes, a multi-course kaiseki dinner and private onsen access. Arrive before dinner service — you'll want every minute.
Days 5–7 · Sun–Tue · Kyoto
Shrines, Geisha & a Thousand Torii
Kyoto is Japan's soul — 1,600 Buddhist temples, Shinto shrines, preserved geisha districts and bamboo groves that filter light into something otherworldly. Three days is barely enough.
06:00
Fushimi Inari at sunriseThousands of vermillion torii gates climbing Mt Inari. The full trail takes 2–3 hours; even the first 30 minutes at dawn is transcendent. You'll have it almost to yourself.
10:00
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove & Tenryu-jiThe bamboo grove is a cliché because it deserves to be. Tenryu-ji's Zen garden reflected in its pond is one of the great garden compositions in Japan.
14:00
Gion districtKyoto's geisha district — cobblestone lanes of wooden machiya townhouses. Hanamikoji street at dusk may yield a sighting of a maiko (apprentice geisha) hurrying to an engagement.
16:30
Kinkaku-ji Golden PavilionStartling gold against dark pines and still water. However many times you've seen it in photographs, the first real view stops you mid-stride.
10:00
Tea ceremony, HigashiyamaA 45-minute matcha ceremony in a machiya townhouse. The ritual preparation is meditative — you'll never drink green tea the same way again.
Days 8–9 · Wed–Thu · Osaka
Japan's Kitchen & the Last Night
Osaka is where Japan loosens its tie. The food is better, the people louder, and the neon of Dotonbori reflects in the canal like a fever dream. Eat everything. Twice.
09:00
Osaka Castle & groundsThe white-walled castle keep over a moat of sakura trees (in spring, staggeringly beautiful). The museum inside covers the Toyotomi clan with genuine drama.
12:00
Kuromon Ichiba MarketOsaka's "Kitchen" — 180 stalls of wagyu skewers, live crabs, fugu sashimi and freshly grilled oysters. Eat as you walk.
15:00
Dotonbori canal & street foodTakoyaki (octopus balls), kushikatsu (breaded skewers) and okonomiyaki (savoury pancake) under the glowing Glico Running Man sign. Eat until you cannot.
20:00
Farewell dinner: Kappo ryoriOsaka-style kappo — counter dining where the chef prepares dishes in front of you, seasonal and spare. A quieter, more refined farewell to Japan than the Dotonbori crowds.

Local tips

💴 Japan is still heavily cash-based. Withdraw yen from 7-Eleven ATMs — they reliably accept foreign cards. Never leave a city without enough cash for at least a day.
🎟 Book teamLab Borderless and popular restaurants weeks ahead. The best omakase counters fill in minutes. Use TableAll or Tablecheck for reservations.
🚇 Tap your IC card (Suica or Pasmo) on every train, subway and bus. Load it at any station machine — it works from Tokyo to Osaka on local trains.
🙏 Remove shoes at ryokan entrances and traditional restaurants. Don't tip — it can cause offence. Both hands when exchanging business cards or receiving gifts.
🍜 Convenience stores (Lawson, 7-Eleven, FamilyMart) serve genuinely excellent food — onigiri, katsu sandwiches, hot oden. Don't be embarrassed; locals eat there too.
