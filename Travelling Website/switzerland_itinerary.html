Day 1 · Wednesday · Zürich
Cobblestones, Chocolate & the Limmat
Zürich surprises visitors who expect only banks and watches. The old town is genuinely beautiful — medieval guildhalls, twin-towered Grossmünster, and a lakefront that glitters in the summer sun.
10:00
Altstadt (Old Town) walkCross the Limmat into Niederdorf — narrow lanes of guildhalls, fountain squares and bookshops. Climb Lindenhügel hill for a view over the red-roofed city to the lake.
11:30
Grossmünster & FraumünsterZürich's two great Romanesque churches face each other across the river. Fraumünster's Chagall stained-glass windows are among Europe's finest — deeply moving in morning light.
13:30
Lunch at Café SprüngliOn Paradeplatz since 1836. The Luxemburgerli macarons are the city's most famous bite — order with a Zürich coffee (espresso, cream, a small chocolate).
16:00
Lake Zürich swimIn summer, the city's Badis (public lake baths) open along the lakeshore. Locals swim in Zürichsee daily — the water is glacier-fed and staggeringly clear.
19:30
Dinner in LangstrasseZürich's most dynamic neighbourhood — Turkish meyhanes, wine bars and the excellent Brasserie Lipp for a proper Swiss rösti and entrecôte.
Day 2 · Thursday · Zürich
Museums, Markets & Fondue
A slower Zürich day — world-class art museums, a market that sells Swiss cheese wheels the size of cartwheels, and your first proper cheese fondue in a centuries-old guild restaurant.
09:30
Kunsthaus ZürichOne of Europe's great art museums — Giacometti's largest permanent collection, Monet, Picasso and a stunning modern wing by David Chipperfield opened in 2021.
12:00
Bürkliplatz Saturday marketThe weekly farmers' market on the lake — regional cheeses, air-dried Bündnerfleisch, Zürich sausages and fresh berries from the Thurgau orchards.
15:00
Uetliberg — Zürich's mountainA 35-minute train to the top of the city's local peak. The panorama stretches from Säntis in the east to the Bernese Alps in the south. Hike the ridge trail back down.
19:30
Fondue at Zunfthaus zur ZimmerleutenA 14th-century guildhall on the Limmat. Order the moitié-moitié (half Gruyère, half Vacherin) fondue and a carafe of Fendant white wine. Do not skip the bread crust at the bottom.
Day 3 · Friday · Lucerne
Chapel Bridge & Pilatus
Lucerne is the most picturesque city in Switzerland — a medieval covered bridge over a mountain lake, a Lion Monument that made Mark Twain weep, and a cogwheel railway to the top of the world.
08:30
Train from Zürich (45 min)One of Europe's prettiest rail journeys — the track hugs Lake Zürich then cuts through green Mittelland farmland. Arrive in Lucerne before the tour groups.
10:00
Kapellbrücke & Old TownThe 14th-century wooden covered bridge with its painted interior panels is Europe's oldest. Walk the intact medieval ramparts for a view of the lake and the Alps beyond.
12:30
Mt Pilatus by Golden Round TripBoat across the lake to Alpnachstad, then the world's steepest cogwheel railway (48° gradient) to the summit at 2,132m. Cable car down to Kriens and bus back to Lucerne.
18:00
Lakefront aperitif & onward to GrindelwaldA last glass of Appenzeller beer on the lakefront, then the Panoramic Express train southeast into the Bernese Oberland.
Days 4–5 · Sat–Sun · Grindelwald
Eiger, Jungfraujoch & the High Alps
The Eiger's north face looms directly above the village. The Jungfraujoch — Europe's highest railway station at 3,454m — delivers you onto an actual glacier. This is why you came to Switzerland.
07:30
Jungfraujoch — Top of EuropeBook the earliest train from Grindelwald. The journey through the Eiger tunnel is remarkable; the Sphinx Observatory at the summit, at 3,571m, looks down onto the Aletsch Glacier — the longest in the Alps.
13:00
Grindelwald First via gondolaThe First Flyer zip line, Trottibike descent and cliff walk above the valley. On a clear day, the Wetterhorn, Schreckhorn and Eiger form an unbroken wall of rock and ice above you.
09:00
Bachalpsee hikeA 4km walk above the gondola station to a mirror-perfect alpine lake reflecting the Wetterhorn. The wildflower meadows in July are extraordinary. Carry a picnic from the village bakery.
19:00
Raclette dinner in the villageAt Restaurant Onkel Tom or similar — half-wheels of Valais raclette melted under the lamp and scraped over boiled potatoes, cornichons and pickled onions. Order twice.
Days 6–7 · Mon–Tue · Zermatt
The Matterhorn & Glacier Paradise
No mountain on earth has a silhouette like the Matterhorn. Zermatt — car-free, cobblestoned, ringed by 38 peaks above 4,000m — is where you understand why people risk their lives to climb mountains.
07:00
Sunrise on the MatterhornWalk up Findeln or take the first lift to Sunnegga (3 min underground funicular). The pyramid catches the first alpine light while the valley is still in shadow — the most iconic view in Switzerland.
10:00
Klein Matterhorn — Glacier ParadiseCable car to 3,883m — Europe's highest cable car station. Walk onto the glacier in summer snow, peer through the tunnel into the glacier cave, and look across to Mont Blanc and the Bernese Alps.
14:00
Five Lakes WalkOne of Switzerland's classic hikes — 4 hours, five mountain lakes each perfectly reflecting the Matterhorn. The Stellisee at golden hour is a photographer's obsession.
19:30
Dinner at Chez VronyA mountain restaurant at 2,000m, accessible by ski gondola even in summer. Swiss farmhouse cooking — Valais lamb, alpine herbs, and a wine list that takes the altitude seriously.
Days 8–9 · Wed–Thu · Geneva
Jet d'Eau, CERN & Lake Leman
Geneva is Switzerland's international face — the UN, the Red Cross, 40,000 diplomats and a jet of water that shoots 140 metres above Europe's largest Alpine lake. It rewards those who look beneath the polish.
10:00
Jet d'Eau & waterfrontThe 140m water cannon on Lake Leman is visible from 40km away. Walk the Quai du Mont-Blanc along the lake — the Alps south of the city form one of Europe's great urban backdrops.
12:00
Vieille Ville (Old Town)Geneva's hilly old town — the Cathédrale Saint-Pierre where Calvin preached, the Place du Bourg-de-Four (the city's oldest square), and the Promenade de la Treille with Europe's longest wooden bench.
14:00
CERN Visitor CentreFree admission to the world's largest particle physics laboratory. The interactive exhibitions on the Large Hadron Collider and the discovery of the Higgs boson are genuinely thrilling — science made visible.
19:00
Farewell dinner: Carouge bistroCross into Carouge — Geneva's Sardinian-built bohemian quarter. Terrace bistros, Genevois sausage with white beans, and a final carafe of Chasselas to toast the mountains you've climbed.

Local tips

🚆 The Swiss Travel Pass covers trains, buses, lake boats and most mountain railways — and entry to 500 museums. Buy it before you arrive; it's the single best investment you can make for this trip.
🏔 Book Jungfraujoch tickets well in advance and aim for an 8am departure. Cloud rolls in by noon almost every day. Clear mornings are not guaranteed — have a flexible day as backup.
🧀 Switzerland has four distinct food cultures: German-Swiss (rösti, wurst, fondue), French-Swiss (raclette, croissants, wine), Italian-Swiss (risotto, polenta) and Romansh. Eat locally wherever you are.
🚗 Zermatt is car-free. Leave your vehicle in Täsch and take the shuttle train (12 min). Within Zermatt, everything is walkable — the village is the point.
💧 Tap water in Switzerland is cleaner than most bottled water. Every public fountain is drinkable. Carry a reusable bottle — you'll fill it from 16th-century stone troughs fed by glacier springs.
