Day 14
Day 1 · Tokyo · Arrival
First Sakura, First Ramen
Land, drop your bags, and walk immediately to the nearest park. The sakura won't wait. Tokyo in full bloom is one of the great urban spectacles on earth.
14:00
Ueno Park — sakura under the canopyJapan's most famous hanami (blossom-viewing) spot. Spread a blue tarp, buy a canned beer from the nearest konbini, and sit under 1,000 cherry trees in full bloom.
17:00
Chidorigafuchi — evening blossom boatsRow a rented pedal boat through a moat tunnel of overhanging sakura. The pink petals fall on the water around you. This is the image you came for.
20:00
Arrival ramen at Fuunji, ShinjukuTsukemen (dipping ramen) from a 12-seat legend. Queue before 7:30pm. The intense pork broth is the most welcoming thing Japan will say to you on day one.
Day 2 · Tokyo · Old City
Senso-ji, Tsukiji & teamLab
Old Tokyo and new Tokyo, ancient ritual and digital hallucination — all within a 20-minute subway ride of each other.
07:00
Senso-ji Temple at dawn with sakuraTokyo's oldest temple draped in cherry blossom. The five-storey pagoda behind a veil of pink petals is among the most photographed sights in Japan — and it earns every frame.
09:30
Tsukiji Outer Market breakfastTamagoyaki, uni rice, fresh oysters and maguro so fresh it barely touches the soy sauce. Japan's greatest breakfast and it costs less than ¥2,000.
14:00
teamLab Borderless, Azabudai HillsThe world's most visited digital art museum. Immersive light rooms where the walls dissolve. Book the first entry slot weeks ahead — it always sells out during cherry blossom season.
Day 3 · Tokyo · Modern City
Shibuya, Harajuku & Golden Gai
The Tokyo of the imagination — crossing floods, fashion tribes, and the greatest concentration of tiny bars on earth. A full day spent losing yourself on purpose.
10:00
Shibuya Sky at opening360° views of Tokyo with Fuji on the horizon. In April, patches of pink sakura dot the city grid below — the view from 230m is extraordinary.
12:30
Harajuku & Meiji JinguFrom Takeshita Street's fashion chaos to the silent cedar forest surrounding Meiji Shrine — Harajuku contains contradictions. Both halves reward the walk.
16:00
Meguro River sakura walkThe river bank is lined with cherry trees whose branches arch over the water. At peak bloom, it's a pink tunnel stretching for 4km. Sakura-flavoured soft serve at every corner.
20:30
Shinjuku Golden Gai200 tiny bars, each seating six, spread across six alleyways. Order whisky, eat whatever the bar puts in front of you, and stay until the city makes you.
Day 5–6 · Hakone
Fuji, Onsen & the Ryokan
The mountain frames everything. Hakone is where you exhale — volcanic steam, tatami floors, a private bath carved from cypress wood, and Fuji appearing and disappearing through the mist.
08:30
Romancecar from ShinjukuPanoramic window limited express through mountains to Hakone in 85 minutes. The front seats look directly through the driver's windshield. Book them.
11:00
Owakudani Volcanic ValleyCable car over sulphur vents and volcanic craters. Eat a kuro-tamago (black egg hard-boiled in volcanic springs). The steam from the earth is primordial and dramatic.
14:00
Lake Ashi — Fuji torii gate viewThe red torii of Hakone Shrine rising from the lake with Fuji behind it — one of the iconic Japan compositions. Clearer in morning light, atmospheric in afternoon mist.
16:00
Ryokan check-in & rotenburo onsenYour outdoor bath overlooks the valley. Soak at dusk, dress in yukata for the kaiseki dinner — twelve seasonal courses served in your room on low lacquer tables.
Days 7–11 · Kyoto · Heart of Japan
Shrines, Geisha & Peak Sakura
Kyoto during cherry blossom peak is the most beautiful place in the world — and everyone knows it. Go early, go late, and find the temple no tour bus reaches. Five days is barely enough.
05:45
Maruyama Park — sakura at dawnThe great weeping cherry tree at the park's centre is 80 years old. At first light, before the crowds, its cascading branches glow pink in the silence. Japan's most famous single tree.
07:30
Fushimi Inari before 8amThe thousand torii tunnels climb Mt Inari for 4km. In the low morning light, the vermillion gates glow and the city spreads below. Walk the full trail; you'll want the silence.
11:00
Philosopher's Path in full bloomA 2km canal walk lined with hundreds of cherry trees whose petals fall like slow pink snow into the water. The most romantic walk in Japan, especially mid-morning when the light is soft.
14:00
Gion & Hanamikoji streetThe geisha district in sakura season — wooden machiya, stone lanterns, a maiko in full kimono walking beneath the cherry trees. This is the image Kyoto keeps for itself.
10:00
Arashiyama — bamboo & blossomThe Bamboo Grove at Tenryu-ji, then the Oi River lined with cherry trees at Togetsu-kyo Bridge. Rent a bicycle and pedal the outer temples — Jojakko-ji for elevated views over blossom-covered rooftops.
15:00
Tea ceremony, Higashiyama machiyaA 45-minute matcha ceremony in a 200-year-old townhouse. Sweets made from sakura petals. The deliberate slowness of the ritual is the entire point.
Days 12–13 · Osaka · Final Act
Japan's Kitchen, Last Night
Osaka is where Japan loosens its tie. The food is better, the people louder, and Osaka Castle in sakura season rivals anything Kyoto puts on. End here — loudly, deliciously.
08:30
Osaka Castle Park in full bloom600 cherry trees surround the moat. The white tenshu castle keep above a sea of pink is Osaka's greatest visual statement. Arrive at opening — it fills fast.
12:00
Kuromon Ichiba MarketOsaka's "Kitchen" — wagyu skewers, live crab, fugu sashimi and oysters grilled to order. Walk slowly, eat everything offered, regret nothing.
15:30
Dotonbori — the city's fever dreamNeon, noise, takoyaki, kushikatsu, okonomiyaki. The Glico Running Man sign reflected in the canal. This is Japan's id — unfiltered and magnificent.
20:00
Final dinner: Kappo ryori counterTwelve courses prepared in front of you — spring vegetables, sakura-smoked fish, wild mountain herbs. A quiet, deeply seasonal farewell to a country that rewards attention.

Local tips

🌸Sakura peak lasts only 7–10 days. Check the Japan Meteorological Corporation forecast the week before you fly — timing everything around it is worth the effort.
💴Japan is still largely cash-based. 7-Eleven ATMs reliably accept foreign cards. Never leave a city without enough yen for a full day.
🎟Book teamLab, Borghese-level restaurants and the Aman weeks ahead. Cherry blossom season is Japan's highest-demand period for everything.
🚇Load a Suica IC card at any station machine. It works on every train, subway and bus from Tokyo to Osaka.
🙏Remove shoes at ryokans. Don't tip — ever. Two hands when receiving anything. These aren't tourist tips; they're the way Japan works.
